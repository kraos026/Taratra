## Scoring audit

No weights were changed. No empirical calibration rationale was found; these are existing catalog/code choices, not measured business truths.

- Business findings contribute severity points: critical 25, high 15, medium 8, low 3, informational 0. Aggregate scores subtract/clamp; health is averaged.
- AI severity scores: 100/75/50/25/10. Complexity: 20/40/60/80/100. Confidence averages finding and evidence confidence. Feasibility: data 35%, confidence 25%, inverse complexity 25%, process confidence 15%. Readiness averages data, feasibility and evidence confidence.
- Automation confidence: with AI, finding 50%, AI 25%, evidence 25%; otherwise finding two-thirds and evidence one-third. Feasibility: connector 35%, presence of evidence 25%, inverse complexity 25%, confidence 15%. Readiness averages coverage, feasibility, connector and confidence.
- ROI confidence: opportunity 50%, evidence presence 25%, assumptions presence 25%. Presence is not the same as reliable measurement.
- Portfolio priority catalog: ROI 30%, business impact 25%, feasibility 15%, inverse complexity 10%, risk 10%, confidence 10%. Thresholds: 85/70/50/30/0. Risk is a positive prioritization input, not a deployment authorization. Rule precedence puts quick wins before compliance. Human approval, per-opportunity prerequisite resolution and organizational readiness are not explicit independent hard gates in this score.
- Blueprint completeness normalization: components 40%, connectors 30%, dependencies 20%, constraints 10%.

ROI arithmetic remains unchanged: saved hours derive from hours per occurrence × annual frequency × volume factor × coverage; benefits add labor and avoided error costs; initial cost combines implementation/training/infrastructure; net annual benefit subtracts maintenance. ROI is `(net - initial) / initial * 100`. Zero investment with positive benefit uses `unbounded`; unrecovered payback remains a special state. Conservative factors are volume 0.75/cost 1.2, expected 1/1, optimistic 1.25/0.9. These factors are assumptions, not confidence intervals.

## Benchmark methodology and limits

The existing `src/brain-evaluation/canonical-shadow-benchmark.ts` creates keyword-based canonical-like snapshots; it does not invoke all canonical domain engines. Its hardcoded confidence values cannot certify the engine. It was not used as evidence of improved canonical decision correctness.

Direct-engine adversarial baseline group 1: 38 assertions, 25 passed / 13 failed. Group 2: 20 assertions, 13 passed / 7 failed. The 20 newly added assertions reproduce the five domain/view defects above; after patches they pass within expanded targeted suites (106 and 27 assertions respectively). The additional parent-selection assertion failed before its repository fix and passes afterward. Expected outcomes exist only in tests, not provider input or runtime logic.

Comparing the exact same assertion names across all three baseline/after pairs gives **46/67 before → 67/67 after**, with no missing assertion. The extended Knowledge suite passes 32/32. The final full Vitest rerun after the parent-selection fix passes 1,153/1,153.

These are regression counts, **not** population rates for unsafe automation, unsupported ROI, contradiction recall or commercial accuracy. No representative before/after unsafe-automation rate, remediation recall, semantic dedup rate or full-stage latency distribution has been established. Those metrics remain NOT MEASURED.

Possible improvements are narrower reads and reduced repeated hydration, but live profiling is required before changing parent selection or transaction boundaries. No timeout increase or speculative parallel transaction work was applied. Unit-test elapsed time is not production latency.
